# Vocabulary data attribution

The NGSL 1.2 and NAWL 1.2 are by Charles Browne, Brent Culligan and Joseph Phillips (2013). NGSL-GR 1.0 is by Browne and Culligan (2024). The current official project site is https://www.newgeneralservicelist.com/ (the linked author-operated pages are hosted at https://charliebrownecompany.squarespace.com/). These lists are licensed under Creative Commons Attribution-ShareAlike 4.0: https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/ .

ROOTLY has adapted the lists by selecting content words, normalising listed spelling variants, combining editorial pilot, topic and school-vocabulary seeds, assigning provisional priorities, and creating five 2,000-entry lists (SSAT Middle, SSAT Upper, TOEFL iBT, TOEFL Junior Standard and IELTS Academic) that share 3,760 entries. Selection and priority are ROOTLY judgements, not official examination lists or measured probabilities. The derived candidate tables and source-based foundation data are released under CC BY-SA 4.0. This data licence does not change the licence of the application's source code or the separately authored ROOTLY pilot passages.

Open English WordNet 2025 is by the Open English Wordnet Team, derived from Princeton University WordNet. Source: https://en-word.net/ and https://github.com/globalwordnet/english-wordnet . OEWN modifications are licensed under CC BY 4.0, with the underlying WordNet licence retained. Full supplied notices are in the accompanying OEWN and WordNet licence files. ROOTLY selects a representative sense for foundation retrieval, preserves source sense/synset IDs, and uses source definitions, available pronunciation data, synonyms and examples. Other senses stay in the source-backed candidate file for subsequent editorial work. Four entries have recorded editorial overrides: one outdated definition (`planet`) is corrected from a primary reference, and three entries (`wound`, `collect`, `spread`) use a chosen representative sense. Each override keeps the source sense ID and its rationale.

Original ROOTLY words and passages retain their existing attribution and review records. The 52 pilot entries and their 104 TOEFL/IELTS passages, the 100 TOEFL Junior reading, meaning and grammar items, and the 240 newer analogy pairs and distractors were authored for this project. Their automatic assembly and the author's content checks are not an independent external linguistic review.
